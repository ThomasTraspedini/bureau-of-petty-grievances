# 0017 — Digital Conduct department

## Status

Accepted.

## Context

Department expansion must make the Bureau materially more expressive without turning communication habits into surveillance, compelled availability, or unrestricted accusation. A second department also tests whether filing, assessment, localized generation, determination presentation, persistence, sharing, and analytics are genuinely department-aware rather than Chronology-specific abstractions with renamed labels.

Digital behavior can be represented through message content, account access, read receipts, presence signals, contact graphs, screenshots, or background observation. Those sources would create disproportionate privacy, consent, and coercion risks for a product intended only for harmless interpersonal irritation. Filer-entered aggregate facts are sufficient to produce specificity while keeping the respondent outside an investigative system.

## Decision

The Department of Digital Conduct is the second enabled end-to-end department. Its first policy version supports exactly three classifications:

- fragmented messages: separate-message count, principal-idea count, and sequence interval;
- excessive voice memorandum: duration and principal-idea count;
- unacknowledged coordination: ordinary response interval and filer follow-up count.

All values are bounded structured facts entered by the filer. The Bureau does not connect to messaging services, inspect message bodies, infer communication metadata, access devices or accounts, observe presence or read receipts, or monitor future responsiveness. The witness statement remains bounded submitted context and is never treated as externally verified evidence.

Digital Conduct owns deterministic severity thresholds, consequence adjustment, mitigation, stable presentation parameters, and three non-binding remedy families: consolidate complete ideas, accompany a voice memorandum with a brief summary, or acknowledge ordinary coordination when practical. Policy prohibits surveillance, compelled availability, assumed urgency, public shaming, exclusion, deprivation, and any health or safety restriction. Remedies cover no more than three future personal exchanges and do not apply to emergencies, work duties, caregiving, access needs, or other serious circumstances.

The determination uses a communications-docket visualization distinct from the Chronology timeline. It is derived only from submitted counts and intervals and explicitly disclaims message, device, and account access. Department-specific English editorial guidance and deterministic fallback share the existing language-neutral determination schema.

Draft and transient-session envelopes advance to version `2` with validated version-1 Chronology migration. Persistent record snapshots remain compatible because their existing discriminated filing boundary can admit the new validated department. Share descriptors advance to version `2`; Digital previews may disclose only the approved aggregate evidence for the classification and mitigation category, never respondent alias, statement, message content, sequence interval, relationship, or impact. Analytics advances to schema version `2` with department and path categories but no submitted fact values.

## Consequences

- Chronology and Digital Conduct complete the same filing-to-public-record lifecycle behind department-discriminated domain and presentation boundaries.
- The communications docket can feel specific without implying external verification or technical access the product does not possess.
- Existing local Chronology work continues through explicit migration rather than silent loss.
- Provider prompts and public previews receive less information than the complete private record; their allowlists remain separate and inspectable.
- Future departments must own their fact grammar, deterministic policy, localized editorial constraints, fallback, visualization, tests, and preview boundary rather than reusing Digital Conduct semantics.

## Open considerations

Domestic Affairs and Social Planning remain required for the Department expansion capability. Any future communication classification involving screenshots, imported content, account connection, workplace performance, safety, caregiving, harassment, or relationship surveillance requires a separate material decision before implementation.

# 0021 — Italian product locale

## Status

Accepted.

## Context

The internationalization boundary requires a public language to own its complete interface copy, deterministic determination language, provider guidance, editorial and safety validation, persistence behavior, and rendered verification before its route is enabled. A translated shell over English determinations would misrepresent the locale of the product and its stored records.

## Decision

Italian (`it`) is the second enabled product locale. It uses a calm, formal `Lei` register and retains the Bureau's sincere administrative voice: humor comes from precise, proportionate institutional attention, never sarcasm, cruelty, threats, or acknowledgement of the joke.

The Italian catalog covers every public interface, metadata, accessibility, recovery, validation, sharing, consultation, and access state. Each of the four departments owns Italian provider instructions, vocabulary anchors, safety constraints, runtime validation, and a complete deterministic fallback behind the shared language schema.

Filing drafts and transient determinations use locale-specific browser keys. Persisted public records admit `en` and `it`, retain an immutable localized snapshot, and never translate an existing determination when the interface locale changes. Provider requests select policy by both locale and department and continue to exclude respondent aliases and exact clock times.

Italian becomes public only with recursive catalog and placeholder checks, department-wide language fixtures, hostile-output rejection, server-boundary tests, accessibility coverage, and a production-rendered route journey. English remains the default and fallback for unsupported routes; it is not substituted inside a valid Italian filing.

## Consequences

- `/it` is a complete product route rather than an interface-only translation.
- Italian records remain Italian wherever they are reopened or shared.
- Editorial policy can evolve independently per locale while the language-neutral schema and domain codes remain shared.
- Every further locale must meet the same release bar and receive its own accepted evaluation evidence before activation.

## Open considerations

French, German, Spanish, and Brazilian Portuguese are now enabled under [decision 0022](0022-multilingual-product-routes.md). Cross-locale translation of an already issued determination is not part of this decision.

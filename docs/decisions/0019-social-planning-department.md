# 0019 — Social Planning department

## Status

Accepted.

## Context

Department expansion must make ordinary planning friction precise without turning the Bureau into a system for monitoring calendars, messages, contacts, locations, attendance, invitations, or social relationships. A fourth complete path also closes the planned department-expansion outcome with a fact and visualization grammar distinct from timekeeping, communications, and household matters.

Social-planning complaints can overlap with serious constraints and interpersonal harm. Safety, health, accessibility, caregiving, employment, finances, travel, law, religious or cultural obligations, harassment, coercion, and exclusion are not petty planning matters and must remain outside Bureau jurisdiction.

## Decision

The Department of Social Planning is the fourth enabled end-to-end department. Its first policy version supports exactly three classifications:

- option-veto cycle: practical options proposed, options rejected, and alternatives offered;
- decision drift: decision rounds, elapsed hours, and participant count;
- post-confirmation revision: revision count, participant count, and notice hours.

Values are bounded structured facts entered by the filer. The Bureau accepts no calendars, messages, contacts, locations, guest lists, invitations, attendance records, social-network data, external observation, or connected-account access. A bounded witness statement remains submitted context rather than verified evidence.

Social Planning owns deterministic severity thresholds, one capped consequence adjustment, required mitigation, stable presentation parameters, and three private non-binding remedy families: a bounded shortlist that pairs rejected choices with alternatives, a clear chooser or reasonable decision point, or clear revision notice with an opt-out. Remedies cover no more than three future low-stakes shared planning decisions. They do not govern serious safety, health, accessibility, caregiving, work, financial, travel, legal, religious, cultural, harassment, coercion, exclusion, attendance, or contact matters.

The determination uses a social decision register distinct from the Chronology timeline, Digital Conduct communications docket, and Domestic Affairs property register. Depending on the classification, it renders an option tree, deliberation history, or revision-impact record from approved submitted counts and intervals and labels the filer as the evidence source. Department-specific English editorial guidance and deterministic fallback share the existing language-neutral determination schema.

Draft and transient-session envelopes advance to version `4` with validated migration of versions 1 through 3. Share descriptors advance to version `4`; Social previews disclose the proposed and rejected option counts, decision rounds with elapsed hours and participant count, or revision count with participant count and notice hours. Option alternatives remain outside the preview. All Social previews exclude respondent alias, witness statement, relationship, impact, and generated prose. Analytics advances to schema version `4` with the Social evidence step and three categorical paths, never submitted fact values. PostgreSQL schema version `6` admits the `social_planning` department and `SOC` reference prefix while retaining existing records.

## Consequences

- Four departments now complete the same filing-to-public-record lifecycle with separate fact and visualization grammars.
- The decision register can feel exact without implying access to a person's calendar, communications, movements, or social graph.
- Existing device-local work and transient determinations continue through explicit migration rather than silent loss.
- Provider, preview, analytics, and persistence boundaries retain separate validated allowlists.
- Department expansion is complete; cross-journey resilience and refinement becomes the next capability.

## Open considerations

Any Social Planning feature involving connected calendars, imported messages, contacts, locations, invitations, attendance, guest lists, social graphs, workplace performance, serious obligations, harassment, coercion, exclusion, or compelled attendance or contact requires a separate material decision before implementation.
